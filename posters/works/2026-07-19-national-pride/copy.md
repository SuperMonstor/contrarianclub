## The motion

> **National pride is a barrier to progress because it discourages
> international comparison.**

Chosen deliberately, and the tradeoff is known: the mechanism clause makes it
the most thought provoking line of the three, and the weaker motion to actually
argue. A debater can accept that pride hurts progress while rejecting that
comparison is why, and win on a technicality.

That is a job for the host. Open by putting the whole motion to the room,
comparison clause included, so the vote is on the claim as written rather than
on the half of it people find easier.

The upside is that the deck now hangs together. The clause names the mechanism,
and slide 5 is that mechanism laid out as a choice, so the argument the poster
promises is the argument the room actually has.

Rejected: `National pride is a barrier to progress`. Cleaner and more postable,
but broad enough that the room spends the night defining "pride".

Rejected: `A country should measure itself against the world's best, not against
its own past`. The best debate, the worst poster. It takes two beats to parse.
It survives as the core conflict on slide 5, which is where it belongs.

## Slide 1: the hook

> **COMING UP NEXT**
>
> # Can too much national pride become a country's biggest weakness?
>
> A question that has divided economists, policymakers and patriots.

The question carries the slide, and the "economists, policymakers and patriots"
line widens it: serious people disagree about this too. "Patriots" is the word
doing the most work. It tells the reader this is about them, not about
macroeconomics.

## Slide 2: the motion

> **THE MOTION**
>
> # National pride is a barrier to progress.
> ## Because it discourages international comparison.
>
> Nobody thinks pride is worthless. The argument is what it costs.

The second line matters. It pre-empts the strawman ("so you hate your country?")
before either side can reach for it, and it tells the reader this will be an
argument between two reasonable positions rather than a pile-on.

The motion is long for a headline. On the slide it wants to break across two
weights: the claim large, the `because` clause smaller underneath it, so the eye
takes the accusation first and the mechanism second.

## Slide 3: for the motion

> **FOR**
>
> ## Look outward.
>
> - The fastest way to improve is to study whoever is doing it better.
> - Pride measures how far we have come. Excellence measures how far we have
>   left to go.
> - Celebrate the distance travelled and you stop running.

## Slide 4: against the motion

> **AGAINST**
>
> ## Look inward.
>
> - Nobody builds a country they are ashamed of.
> - Pride is what makes people stay, contribute, and believe the thing can work.
> - Measure yourself only against the rich and you learn resentment, not
>   ambition.

## Slide 5: the core conflict

The best slide. Kept close to the original.

> **THE CORE CONFLICT**
>
> ## Where should the standard come from?
>
> **Its own journey**
> - Respects history and context.
> - Builds unity and commitment.
> - Measures how far we have come.
>
> **Or the world's best**
> - Sets a higher benchmark.
> - Forces learning.
> - Measures how far we still have to go.

## Slide 6: the room

The format line is the sell. It says this is an argument you take part in, not a
panel you watch.

> **NEXT DEBATE**
>
> # National pride is a barrier to progress
> ## Because it discourages international comparison.

> The side that moves the most minds wins.

The claim is huge, the mechanism sits under it, same break as slide 2. The vote
line was cut to its second half: "You vote before. You vote after." is implied
by "moves the most minds", and the shorter line survives the centered closing
lockup without wrapping.

Note on the time: the slot runs 2:00 pm to 5:00 pm, but the poster says "2:00 pm
onwards" the way the last one did. A three hour window on a poster reads like a
commitment to sit through three hours. The full window still belongs on the
Offlyn listing, where people are already committing.

## Voice rules for this carousel

Short declaratives, dry and a little ceremonial. No em dashes, no exclamation
marks, no emoji, no ellipses. One idea per slide: if a slide needs a third
paragraph, it is two slides.
