# Carousel: national pride and progress

Working copy for the next debate's Instagram carousel. Edit this until the
words are right, then it becomes `spec.ts` in this folder.

## Decisions, all settled

- **India stays unnamed.** The copy keeps it abstract; the room makes it
  concrete.
- **The two-column compare became the `versus` core template**, general to any
  two-sided slide.
- **Every slide carries art, chosen for what that slide says**, not for period
  flavour. No panning across one painting: the pictures argue alongside the
  words.
- **The interior is prose, not bullets.** It should read as one argument spoken
  aloud, each sentence handing off to the next.
- **Art, all public domain via Wikimedia Commons, in `assets/`:** Delacroix
  (Liberty) opens, Bruegel (Babel) holds the motion, then Rembrandt (Anatomy
  Lesson), Bruegel (Cockaigne), Hals (Banquet), Ford Madox Brown (Work),
  Bruegel (Harvesters), Van Gogh (Sorrowing Old Man), Rembrandt (Syndics), and
  Friedrich (Wanderer) closes.

The built carousel is `spec.ts`; renders are in `out/`. This file records the

## The motion

> **National pride is a barrier to progress because it discourages
> international comparison.**

Chosen deliberately, and the tradeoff is known: the mechanism clause makes it
the most thought provoking line of the three, and the weaker motion to actually
argue. A debater can accept that pride hurts progress while rejecting that
comparison is why, and win on a technicality.

That is a job for the host. Open by putting the whole motion to the room,
comparison clause included, so the vote is on the claim as written rather than
on the half of it people find easier.

The upside is that the deck now hangs together. The clause names the mechanism,
and slide 5 is that mechanism laid out as a choice, so the argument the poster
promises is the argument the room actually has.

Rejected: `National pride is a barrier to progress`. Cleaner and more postable,
but broad enough that the room spends the night defining "pride".

Rejected: `A country should measure itself against the world's best, not against
its own past`. The best debate, the worst poster. It takes two beats to parse.
It survives as the core conflict on slide 5, which is where it belongs.

## Slide 1: the hook

> **COMING UP NEXT**
>
> # Can too much national pride hold a country back?
>
> A question that has divided economists, policymakers and patriots.

The question carries the slide, and the "economists, policymakers and patriots"
line widens it: serious people disagree about this too. "Patriots" is the word
doing the most work. It tells the reader this is about them, not about
macroeconomics.

## Slide 2: the motion

> **THE MOTION**
>
> # National pride is a barrier to progress.
> ## Because it discourages international comparison.
>
> Nobody thinks pride is worthless. The argument is what it costs.

The second line matters. It pre-empts the strawman ("so you hate your country?")
before either side can reach for it, and it tells the reader this will be an
argument between two reasonable positions rather than a pile-on.

The motion is long for a headline. On the slide it wants to break across two
weights: the claim large, the `because` clause smaller underneath it, so the eye
takes the accusation first and the mechanism second.

## Slides 3 to 5: The Case For

Prose, not bullets. Each slide is a sentence that hands off to the next, and
each painting is chosen for the sentence it sits under.

> 1. Start here. The fastest way to get better at anything is to study whoever
>    is already doing it better.
>    *(Rembrandt, The Anatomy Lesson: students crowding in to learn from a master.)*
> 2. But pride quietly changes what you measure. You start judging yourself by
>    how far you have come, not by how far you still have to go.
>    *(Bruegel, The Land of Cockaigne: men lying stuffed and idle.)*
> 3. And once that happens, you celebrate progress instead of chasing
>    excellence. You stop running.
>    *(Hals, Banquet of the Officers: toasting themselves.)*

## Slides 6 to 8: The Case Against

It answers back rather than starting over. "Then again" is doing the work.

> 1. Then again, nobody builds a country they are ashamed of.
>    *(Ford Madox Brown, Work: people building the thing they believe in.)*
> 2. Pride is what makes people stay, contribute, and believe the thing can
>    actually work.
>    *(Bruegel, The Harvesters: a country fed by its own hands.)*
> 3. And if you only ever measure yourself against the rich, you do not learn
>    ambition. You learn resentment, and the quiet belief that you will never
>    catch up.
>    *(Van Gogh, Sorrowing Old Man.)*

## Slide 9: the core conflict

Put as a question, with each side answered in one sentence rather than a
bullet list.

> **THE CORE CONFLICT**
>
> ## So where should the standard come from?
>
> **Its own journey.** It respects where we started, it holds people together,
> and it measures how far we have come.
>
> **The world's best.** It sets a higher bar, it forces us to learn, and it
> measures how far we still have to go.
>
> *Come and settle it.*

## Slide 10: the room

The format line is the sell. It says this is an argument you take part in, not a
panel you watch.

> **NEXT DEBATE**
>
> # National pride is a barrier to progress
> ## Because it discourages international comparison.
>
> | | |
> | --- | --- |
> | Date | Sunday, 19 July |
> | Time | 1:00 pm onwards |
> | Where | Underground Comedy Club, Koramangala |
> | Tickets | On Offlyn |
>
> The side that moves the most minds wins.

The claim is huge, the mechanism sits under it, same break as slide 2. The vote
line was cut to its second half: "You vote before. You vote after." is implied
by "moves the most minds", and the shorter line survives the centered closing
lockup without wrapping.

Note on the time: the poster says "1:00 pm onwards" rather than printing an end
time. A hard window on a flyer reads like a commitment to sit through the whole
thing, which costs casual yeses. The full window belongs on the Offlyn listing,
where people are already committing.

The because-clause is not a footnote. It is the half of the claim the debate
actually turns on, so on the slide it is set in the same serif as the motion, in
ivory rather than gold: gold lost against the pale sky behind it.

## Voice rules for this carousel

Short declaratives, dry and a little ceremonial. No em dashes, no exclamation
marks, no emoji, no ellipses. One idea per slide: if a slide needs a third
paragraph, it is two slides.
