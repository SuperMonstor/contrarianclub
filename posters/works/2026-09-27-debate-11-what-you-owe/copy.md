# Debate Club #11

Sunday 27 September, 2 to 5 pm, Big Pitcher, Indiranagar.

The club is trying the reverse of Debate #10 here. #10 printed its first motion
verbatim on the poster and spent eight slides arguing it. This one announces
the theme and holds both motions back until the room.

## The frame

The theme is **What can India ask of you?** "Can" carries both readings the
room will argue: what India is able to demand of a private life, and what it
is entitled to. Each motion is one such demand, and the poster names only what
each would be for:

- I. For India's image
- II. For India's future

Everything published before the night stays at that level. The poster says
nothing about festivals or children on purpose, and so does everything below:
the paintings (Bruegel's carnival, Reymerswaele's tax collectors) are the only
hint, and a reader who works it out from them has earned it.

## Listing title

**The Contrarian Debate Club #11: What Can India Ask Of You?**

Alternates:

- Debate Club #11: What Can India Ask Of You?
- Debate Club #11: Two Motions, Revealed On The Day

The first is the recommendation, and the ordering follows #9's: the edition
number does the returning-crowd work, the theme does the selling, and "Debate
Club" is the phrase someone is actually searching for. Use the short second
form anywhere the organiser name already shows next to the title. The last is
the one to switch to if sales are slow and the withholding needs to become
the hook.

## The motions, held back

Not for publication before the room. These are the full texts, and they are
what goes on the handout and the recap, unchanged:

1. This Club Believes That Indians Should Sacrifice Public Festival
   Celebrations Abroad To Protect India's Global Image.
2. This Club Would Impose a Higher Income Tax on Adults Who Voluntarily Choose
   to Remain Childfree.

The repo's rule is that a motion prints verbatim and is never compressed into
a slogan. That rule is not suspended by this poster, it simply does not apply:
the poster prints a theme instead of motions. When these two reach the
handout, the ticket page after the night, or a recap, they go in word for word.

Note the asymmetry when writing anything longer for this night. The first
motion asks for a sacrifice, freely made. The second does not ask: it imposes.
"What can India ask of you?" holds both, but a carousel or a recap should say
the difference out loud rather than smooth it over. It is the most interesting
thing about the pairing, and probably where the room splits.

## Open questions

- **Tickets.** The poster says "Tickets out now". If the link is not live when
  this posts, change it to "Tickets open Thursday" or similar rather than
  leaving it as is.
- **Format detail.** The poster says debaters are drawn at random and the
  floor opens afterwards, which is what is settled. If #11 runs the Open Floor
  mechanics (two teams, fifteen minutes to prepare, statements then rebuttals),
  the listing can carry them: they make the night sound like a format rather
  than an open mic.
- **The carousel.** Planned separately. The poster is deliberately the whole
  offer in one frame, so the carousel can open on it and then take each half
  in turn, still without naming either motion.
