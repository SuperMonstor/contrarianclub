# Debate Club #11

Sunday 27 September, 2 to 5 pm, Big Pitcher, Indiranagar.

The club is trying the reverse of Debate #10 here. #10 printed its first motion
verbatim on the poster and spent eight slides arguing it. This one announces
the themes and holds both motions back until the room, so the listing and the
caption carry more weight than usual: they are the only places a reader gets
more than two sentences about what the night is.

## Listing title

**The Contrarian Debate Club #11: What Do You Owe The Country?**

Alternates:

- Debate Club #11: What Do You Owe The Country?
- Debate Club #11: Private Lives, National Interest
- Debate Club #11: Two Motions, Announced On The Day

The first is the recommendation, and the ordering is the same as #9's: the
edition number does the returning-crowd work, the question does the selling,
and "Debate Club" is the phrase someone is actually searching for. Use the
short second form anywhere the organiser name is already shown next to the
title. The fourth is the one to switch to if sales are slow and the
withholding needs to become the hook.

## Description

> The Contrarian Debate Club returns for its eleventh edition.
>
> The theme is what the country is allowed to ask of you. Two motions sit
> under that question. One is about Indians abroad and the image they carry
> home. The other is about choosing not to have children, and who ends up
> paying for that choice.
>
> Both motions are announced on the day. Nobody sees them in advance, so
> nobody turns up with a prepared case, and a first timer stands on the same
> ground as a regular.
>
> Debaters are drawn at random. Put your name in, and if it comes out you are
> arguing. Once the teams have had their say the floor opens, so anyone in the
> room can take it from there. Plenty of the room only ever listens, and that
> is a perfectly good way to spend an afternoon.
>
> Seats are limited on purpose. A small room is the only way everyone who
> wants to speak gets to.
>
> Sunday, 27 September, 2 to 5 pm. Big Pitcher, Indiranagar.

## The motions, held back

Not for publication before the room. These are the full texts, and they are
what goes on the handout and the recap, unchanged:

1. This Club Believes That Indians Should Sacrifice Public Festival
   Celebrations Abroad To Protect India's Global Image.
2. This Club Would Impose a Higher Income Tax on Adults Who Voluntarily Choose
   to Remain Childfree.

The repo's rule is that a motion prints verbatim and is never compressed into
a slogan. That rule is not suspended by this poster, it simply does not apply:
the poster prints themes instead of motions. When these two reach the handout,
the ticket page after the night, or a recap, they go in word for word.

## Open questions

- **Tickets.** The poster says "Tickets out now". If the link is not live when
  this posts, change it to "Tickets open Thursday" or similar rather than
  leaving it as is.
- **Format detail.** The poster says debaters are drawn at random and the
  floor opens afterwards, which is what is settled. If #11 runs the Open Floor
  mechanics (two teams, fifteen minutes to prepare, statements then rebuttals),
  the listing can carry them: they make the night sound like a format rather
  than an open mic.
- **The carousel.** Planned separately. The poster is deliberately the whole
  offer in one frame, so the carousel can open on it and then argue each theme
  without repeating anything.
