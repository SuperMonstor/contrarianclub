# Debate Club #11

Sunday 27 September, 2 to 5 pm, Big Pitcher, Indiranagar.

The club is trying the reverse of Debate #10 here. #10 printed its first motion
verbatim on the poster and spent eight slides arguing it. This one announces
the themes and holds both motions back until the room, so the listing and the
caption carry more weight than usual: they are the only places a reader gets
more than two sentences about what the night is.

## The frame

The theme is sacrifice, because the word is in the first motion and because
both motions are the same demand: the country wants something a private person
would rather keep. One wants a quieter festival, for India's image. The other
wants money, for India's future. The phrase normally means dying in a war, and
here it means a tax bracket, which is the joke and should not be explained
anywhere it appears.

## Listing title

**The Contrarian Debate Club #11: What Should You Sacrifice For India?**

Alternates:

- Debate Club #11: What Should You Sacrifice For India?
- Debate Club #11: What Is India Allowed To Ask Of You?
- Debate Club #11: Two Motions, Announced On The Day

The first is the recommendation, and the ordering follows #9's: the edition
number does the returning-crowd work, the question does the selling, and
"Debate Club" is the phrase someone is actually searching for. Use the short
second form anywhere the organiser name already shows next to the title. The
last is the one to switch to if sales are slow and the withholding needs to
become the hook.

## The motions, held back

Not for publication before the room. These are the full texts, and they are
what goes on the handout and the recap, unchanged:

1. This Club Believes That Indians Should Sacrifice Public Festival
   Celebrations Abroad To Protect India's Global Image.
2. This Club Would Impose a Higher Income Tax on Adults Who Voluntarily Choose
   to Remain Childfree.

The repo's rule is that a motion prints verbatim and is never compressed into
a slogan. That rule is not suspended by this poster, it simply does not apply:
the poster prints themes instead of motions. When these two reach the handout,
the ticket page after the night, or a recap, they go in word for word.

Note the asymmetry when writing anything else for this night. The first motion
asks for a sacrifice, freely made. The second does not ask: it imposes. The
poster holds them together under one word by reading the tax as what you pay
for the sacrifice you declined, which is fair to both, but a longer piece
should say the difference out loud rather than smooth it over. It is the most
interesting thing about the pairing.

## Open questions

- **Tickets.** The poster says "Tickets out now". If the link is not live when
  this posts, change it to "Tickets open Thursday" or similar rather than
  leaving it as is.
- **How much theme two gives away.** "What you never spent on children" says
  the argument is about money without naming the mechanism. The sharper line,
  "Your money, if you choose not to have children", was passed over as too
  close to the motion. If the softer line reads as vague in the feed, that is
  the one to switch to.
- **Format detail.** The poster says debaters are drawn at random and the
  floor opens afterwards, which is what is settled. If #11 runs the Open Floor
  mechanics (two teams, fifteen minutes to prepare, statements then rebuttals),
  the listing can carry them: they make the night sound like a format rather
  than an open mic.
- **The carousel.** Planned separately. The poster is deliberately the whole
  offer in one frame, so the carousel can open on it and then argue each theme
  without repeating anything.
