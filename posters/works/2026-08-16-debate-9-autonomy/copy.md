# Debate Club #9

The poster carries no date, no venue and no motions. Date and time live in the
booking portal, and the motions are held back until the room so nobody arrives
with a prepared case. That puts real weight on the listing and the caption:
they are the only places a reader learns when this is.

## Listing title

**Debate Club #9: Personal Autonomy vs Social Obligation**

Use the full club name in the title if the platform does not show the
organiser prominently:

- The Contrarian Debate Club #9: Personal Autonomy vs Social Obligation

Alternates:

- Personal Autonomy vs Social Obligation: Debate Club #9
- Debate Club #9: Two Motions, Revealed in the Room

The first is the recommendation. The edition number does the returning-crowd
work and the axis does the selling, in that order, because a listing page is
scanned left to right and "Debate Club" is the word someone is searching for.
The third is the one to use if ticket sales are slow and the withholding needs
to become the hook.

## Description

> The Contrarian Debate Club returns for its ninth edition.
>
> The theme is personal autonomy against social obligation. Two motions on
> where one ends and the other begins: how much of your life belongs to the
> people who raised you, and how much of it stays yours when you are the one
> doing the damage.
>
> You will not see the motions before the night. Nobody will. That way nobody
> turns up with a prepared case, and a first timer stands on the same ground
> as a regular.
>
> Debaters are drawn at random. Put your name in, and if it comes out you are
> arguing. Once the teams have had their say the floor opens, so anyone in the
> room can take it from there. Plenty of the room only ever listens, and that
> is a perfectly good way to spend the evening.
>
> Seats are limited on purpose. A small room is the only way everyone who
> wants to speak gets to.
>
> <date and time>

## The motions, held back

Not for publication. These are what gets revealed in the room.

1. This Club Believes that Indian society places too much emphasis on
   familial obligation at the expense of individual autonomy.
2. Respecting personal autonomy requires society to tolerate self
   destructive choices.

## Open questions

- **Date, time and venue.** Off the poster by decision. The listing and the
  caption both have a placeholder that has to be filled before publishing.
- **Format detail.** The listing says debaters are drawn at random and the
  floor opens afterwards, because that is what is settled. If #9 keeps the
  Open Floor mechanics (two teams of two to three, fifteen minutes to
  prepare, a round of statements then a round of rebuttals), add them: they
  make the night sound like a format rather than an open mic.
- **Assigned sides.** Not stated, because it is not confirmed whether drawn
  debaters argue the side they are given or the side they hold. Worth saying
  either way, since it changes who puts their name in.
- **Saying the withholding out loud on the poster.** The poster does not
  currently say the motions are held back. A line like "Motions revealed in
  the room" would turn an omission into part of the format.
