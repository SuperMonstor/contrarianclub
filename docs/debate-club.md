# The Contrarian Debate Club

A members' salon for argument — a place to think out loud, change your mind in
public, and defend the unpopular view without apology. This document is the
reference for what the club is and how each of our events runs. For the visual
system see [`brand-guide.md`](./brand-guide.md); for the live app see the
root [`README.md`](../README.md).

---

## What we're about

We run live debate nights where the audience is part of the event, not a
spectator. Everyone votes on the motion before and after, questions are taken
from the floor, and — depending on the format — anyone in the room might end up
arguing. The goal is good-faith disagreement: sharp on the ideas, generous with
the people.

Every event follows the same spine:

1. **A motion or topic** is put to the room.
2. **A pre-debate vote** captures where the room stands.
3. **The debate** runs in one of the formats below.
4. **A post-debate vote** captures where the room landed.
5. **The opinion swing** — the change between the two votes — is the result we
   care about. Winning isn't "most applause," it's "most minds moved."

## Formats

We run three formats. The first two are our mainline public events; the third is
an invite-only community night.

### 1. Open Debate

The free-flowing format. Two debaters hold the floor on opposite sides of a
motion, but the seats aren't fixed — debaters **swap in and out**, and **anyone
in the audience can step up to rebut**. The floor is genuinely open: if you
think the current speaker is wrong, you can take their place and make the case
yourself.

- **Who speaks:** two active debaters at any moment, continuously rotating.
- **Audience role:** can jump in to rebut and take a debating seat.
- **Feels like:** a live, churning argument where the lineup never settles.
- **Best for:** high energy, wide participation within a single motion.

### 2. Keynote Debate

The marquee format. Two **primary speakers** drive the entire conversation from
start to finish — they carry the argument, and the debate belongs to them. The
audience participates through a **dedicated Q&A section** rather than by taking
the floor.

- **Who speaks:** two keynote debaters for the full debate.
- **Audience role:** submits and asks questions in a structured Q&A segment.
- **Feels like:** a headline bout — polished, sustained, two strong voices.
- **Best for:** depth on a single motion and showcasing our strongest debaters.

### 3. Open House *(invite-only)*

> **Our house. Open floor.** — community debates.

Our community night, built for the people who don't usually get the mic. Each
mainline event only has room for about six people to actively debate; Open House
exists to widen that number dramatically and give more members a real shot.

- **Access:** invite-only. Open to **regulars** who attend the clubs
  consistently and to people who have **actively applied to be a debater**. The
  "open" in Open House is the *floor*, not the door — it's our house, and the
  guest list is the community.
- **Topics:** we run **two to three topics, decided on the spot** rather than
  announced in advance. Lighter prep, faster turns, more surprise.
- **Emphasis:** heavy weighting toward **open-floor segments** and giving
  **more people the opportunity to debate**. Reps over polish. The point is to
  get newer and aspiring debaters on their feet in a low-stakes room.
- **Feels like:** a community meetup where everyone argues — warmer and more
  participatory than the mainline nights.
- **Best for:** developing debaters, building the community, and finding the
  next set of keynote speakers.

## Format at a glance

| | Open Debate | Keynote Debate | Open House |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Audience** | Public | Public | Invite-only (regulars + applicants) |
| **Who debates** | Rotating; audience can step in | Two fixed keynote speakers | Many — as many as possible |
| **Audience role** | Rebut / take the floor | Structured Q&A | Heavy open-floor participation |
| **Topics** | One motion | One motion | 2–3, decided on the spot |
| **Emphasis** | Energy & rotation | Depth & showcase | Access & community |

## Roles at an event

- **Host** — runs the night: sets the motion, opens and closes votes, drives the
  presenter display, keeps time.
- **Moderator** — manages the floor and the Q&A queue; approves, orders, and
  pins audience questions.
- **Debaters** — argue the motion. Fixed in Keynote, rotating in Open Debate,
  and as many as we can field in Open House.
- **Audience** — votes before and after, asks questions, and (format depending)
  takes the floor.
- **Presenter display** — the projected view showing the motion, join QR/code,
  live votes, the opinion swing, timers, and Q&A.

## A note on names

- **Open Debate** and **Keynote Debate** are the two mainline public formats.
- **Open House** is the invite-only community format — tagline *"Our house. Open
  floor."*, described plainly as *community debates*. The name deliberately reads
  as an open *floor* (everyone speaks), not an open *door* (anyone walks in);
  the invitation is what makes it a community.
